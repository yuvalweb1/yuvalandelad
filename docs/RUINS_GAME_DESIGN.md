# THE RUINS OF [GROUPNAME] — Chaos Timeline redesign (v5)

> Design document for replacing Chaos Timeline (the guess-then-reveal quiz) with a
> top-down exploration/deduction mini-game. Written before implementation; shipped
> code follows this spec where practical, with phasing decided per-session (see
> project memory `project_ruins_kingdom.md` for phase status).

## Context

Chaos Timeline is generated from the **entire history** of a group chat. The current implementation ([src/views/ChaosTimeline.jsx](c:/workspace/whatsappRecap/src/views/ChaosTimeline.jsx)) is a guessing quiz — still a consumption loop: *system shows next thing, user reacts*. The brief asks for a full replacement: a mini game with a directly controlled character, where the group's history is an explorable world.

**v5 revision directive (from review):** keep the kingdom exploration, restoration loop, and Procession finale — but the core loop must be *active investigation*, not walking-and-reading. Players must **earn** discoveries through deduction, interrogation, and faction navigation. Optimize for "one more discovery / one more mystery" compulsion.

Grounding constraints (from codebase exploration):
1. **Determinism invariant** — no LLM, no network, no randomness. Same chat → same world. All fiction generated by deterministic rules from computable signals.
2. **Honest signal inventory** — analytics today provides: chaos peaks **with real message excerpts**, per-day seismogram, eras, dead zones, bursts, revivals/kills, per-user stats (top words/emojis, night , response times…). It does **not** detect arguments/betrayals/alliances — the lore system maps fiction onto computable proxies and flags the 4 new deterministic detectors needed.
3. The app already ships **The Long Run** (linear canvas runner, duo mode): proves the 360×560 canvas, seeded-PRNG, localStorage-progression pattern. The chaos game must occupy different verbs: *deduction, exploration, mystery* — not reflexes.

## 1. Why Timeline Products Fail

1. **No agency** — the only verb is "next."
2. **No mystery** — conclusions arrive before curiosity ("Biggest fight: March 2023"). Games show the burnt village first and explain later.
3. **Chronology is the least interesting ordering of memories** — memory is spatial/associative, not sequential.
4. **No cost = no value** — a fact handed over is cheap; the same fact *deduced* is a trophy.
5. **Data framing kills emotion** — charts put the brain in analyst mode; nostalgia needs places, characters, consequences.
6. **No state, no stakes** — a timeline is identical before and after reading.
7. **Single-session by design** — nothing hidden, so nothing left to want.

**Design tests:** every insight must be (a) deduced or earned, never delivered; (b) anchored to a place; (c) wrapped in fiction whose explanation is the real event; (d) gated behind genuine player effort; (e) missable — so finding it means something.

## 4–5. Iteration History

- **v1 — Walkable Museum.** Walk to monuments, tap to read. *Rejected:* a timeline you walk through.
- **v2 — Mystery-first + shards.** Monuments unexplained; collect shards to unlock the story. *Rejected:* collection ≠ deduction; still passive at the reveal.
- **v3 — Central mystery + knowledge gates.** "The Great Silence" main quest; vault doors test attention. *Better:* a spine exists. *Failure:* world static, moment-to-moment loop still walk-collect-read.
- **v4 — Restoration + living world + Procession finale.** Solving permanently relights the kingdom; finale parade. *Failure (review):* emotionally strong, not addictive — risks "walking simulator with collectibles." Too much reading, not enough earning.
- **v5 (this design) — The Verdict Engine.** Keep world/restoration/Procession; replace the core loop with an active deduction game: every monument is a **Case** the player must *solve* — assembling evidence, cross-examining rival faction testimony, reconstructing scenes, and committing falsifiable verdicts. Reading is replaced by deducing; reveals are graded; every solved case spawns new leads.

## 6. Final Game Vision

**Title (working):** *The Ruins of [GroupName]*.

**Pitch:** Your group chat is a fallen kingdom and you are **The Latecomer** — newest member, handed a corrupted archive and an empty case book. The kingdom's Heart is dark, its records sealed, and its two rival factions tell different stories about everything. Walk the ruins, dig up evidence, catch the echoes lying, and *prove* what really happened at every scorched plaza — because in this kingdom, **truth is literally power**: every verdict you get right restores the world. Solve your way to the one question nobody will answer — what caused The Great Silence — and relight the Heart.

**Fantasy:** "I finally scrolled up and read the lore" → upgraded to "I *cracked* the lore." For veterans: "I was there — I can solve this from memory" (and the game grades them on it).

**Tone:** Mock-epic noir. Solemn detective gravitas about utterly mundane chat events; the comedy is the gap, and the real excerpts puncture the epic register with reality.

**Session shape:** cases run 2–4 minutes each; main arc 20–30 min across 8–12 cases; built for one-thumb portrait play in the 360×560 frame.

## 6b. THE CORE LOOP — The Verdict Engine

The minute-to-minute game. Everything else hangs off this.

### The Case cycle (2–4 minutes, repeatable, escalating)

```
LEAD → SCENE → EVIDENCE (3 active verbs) → VERDICT → GRADED REVEAL → RESTORATION + 1-2 NEW LEADS
```

1. **Lead.** The case book points somewhere ("Witnesses speak of a fire in the Second Age — find the plaza"). Travel there; navigation is short and purposeful.
2. **Scene.** The monument poses concrete questions as **empty verdict slots**: *Who ignited it? __ · What hour? __ · Who had the last word? __ · What was the sacred word? __* (3–5 slots per case, all deterministically checkable from peak/user data). Locked slots create instant itch — the Zeigarnik hook.
3. **Evidence — three active verbs, rotated so no case plays the same:**
   - **Excavate:** brush-out minigame at sparkle sites → physical evidence (a half-burnt fragment of a real excerpt with the author missing; a sundial shadow fixing the hour; a tally-stone giving counts).
   - **Interrogate:** echo NPCs near the scene give 2–3 testimony lines. Testimonies are generated from real data — but **rival-faction echoes shade the truth** (deterministic distortion: exaggerate their side's counts, omit their side's caps-lock). Pressing an echo with a contradicting evidence item makes it concede a corrected line. Catching a lie is its own scored event.
   - **Reconstruct:** the scene's ghost figures replay the peak as silhouettes with **author-less speech bubbles** (real excerpts). The player drags member-medallions onto bubbles, deducing authorship from style tells the game has taught them (this member's top emoji, that one's all-caps habit, the one who only sends voice notes). Correct placements snap with satisfying feedback; this converts *reading the excerpts* into *solving the excerpts* — the player studies every line because authorship is the puzzle.
4. **Verdict.** Open the case board and commit answers to all slots. Committing is deliberately weighty: a stamp animation, no take-backs this visit.
5. **Graded reveal.** Slots flip one by one (slot-machine pacing): ✓ gold / ~ close / ✗ wrong, then the full true scene plays — the real messages, real names, real time — now *felt as a payoff*, because the player has skin in every line. Grade = case seal (Gold/Silver/Bronze). Gold seals on first attempt are rare and brag-worthy.
6. **Restoration + leads.** Any solved case (even Bronze) restores the monument — lanterns, bells, echo-figures return; the Heart's glow ticks up. The reveal **always ends by opening 1–2 new leads** ("The arsonist was seen again, three winters later, in the Night District…" — generated from that member's other peaks). The case book never empties; the next mystery is always already itching.

### Why this is compulsive (the addiction architecture, honestly engineered)

- **Open loops everywhere:** empty verdict slots, unstamped cases, silhouetted codex pages, locked vaults — the UI is a wall of almost-closed loops (Zeigarnik effect).
- **Variable reward:** grade uncertainty at every stamp; rare Gold seals; occasional jackpot evidence (a "pristine relic" = an uncut legendary excerpt).
- **Cliffhanger economy:** reveals end in new leads, never in closure — closure is reserved for the finale.
- **Short cycles, visible progress:** 2–4 min per case, Heart-glow meter and map-ink always advancing.
- **Competence curve:** players genuinely *get better* (learning members' styles improves Reconstruct accuracy) — mastery, not grinding.
- **Honest design (per app values):** no fake urgency, no timers, no pay/ads inside the loop; the pull is curiosity and mastery, and quitting mid-case is always cleanly resumable.

### Anti-walking-simulator guarantees
- No case is solvable by walking and reading alone — every slot requires at least one active verb.
- Travel legs capped (~30s) and always carry a live purpose (an open lead).
- Flavor text exists, but *information needed for verdicts* never appears as plain readable text — it must be excavated, extracted from a pressed witness, or deduced in reconstruction.

## 7. World Design

One continuous top-down kingdom (3–6 districts by chat size), fog-of-war until walked, camera-follow, 360×560.

- **Gates & Outskirts** — 90-second tutorial case (one slot, one verb each) ending in the first restoration and three new leads fanning into the map.
- **Era Districts** — generated from era stats: message density → building density; night share → nocturnal palette; media share → murals; voice share → bells; dominant members → whose statues stand in the square.
- **Monuments = Cases** — one per chaos peak (top 10, all with real excerpts). Form follows composition: caps-heavy → scorched; emoji-heavy → carnival ruin; many senders → amphitheater; voice-heavy → bell tower; late-night → observatory.
- **The Ashen Reach** — the longest dead zone as a wasteland splitting the kingdom; the stopped clocktower is the meta-case terminal.
- **Faction Marches** *(new detector)* — contested borderland between the two most-clashing members' territories: rival banners, rival testimony, the trust system's home turf.
- **Joke Shrines** *(new detector)* — shrines to recurring phrases/emojis; each holds a micro-case ("decode the sacred word" — fill-in-the-blank from distributed clues).
- **The Heart** — central dark beacon; glow = global restoration meter; finale stage.

Deterministic layout seeded from the chat hash (mulberry32, as in The Long Run). Same export → same kingdom forever: it becomes "ours," referenceable ("meet me at the Falafel War memorial").

## 8. Character Design

- **The Latecomer (player):** silent detective-archaeologist; lantern (discovery radius), brush (excavation), case book (the meta-UI).
- **The Archivist (guide):** owl-ish keeper; issues leads, grades verdicts, dry commentary. Template dialogue with `interp()` slots only.
- **Legendary Figures (per significant member):** statue + interrogable echo, archetype by dominant stat (deterministic, same spirit as existing titles/roasts): most revivals → **The Lightbringer**; most kills → **The Closer**; night owl → **The Sentinel**; voice-heavy → **The Herald**; top emoji → **The Illuminator**; longest absence → **The Vanished** (empty plinth = hidden case); burst champion → **The Stormcaller**. Echo speech is seasoned with the member's real top words/emojis — and those tells are *gameplay*, because Reconstruct authorship deduction depends on learning them.
- **Faction leaders:** the two echoes at the heart of the detected feud anchor the trust system; each insists their side "won."

Lore-voice rules: fiction never lies about facts; every verdict answer is backed by evidence (existing `evidence` discipline); faction *distortions* are deterministic and always correctable by hard evidence; all strings via i18n templates (en.js first, he.js + RTL).

## 10. Exploration & Faction Systems

- **Movement:** virtual joystick / drag (touch) + arrows/WASD; districts cross in ~40s.
- **Fog of memory:** world inks in permanently as walked; the map is itself a progress artifact and share-bait.
- **Leads as navigation:** the case book lists open leads with district-level hints (never waypoints) — finding the scene is part of the game; the lantern sparkles only near relevant sites.
- **Faction trust (the navigate-rivals system):** helping a faction's cases (or stamping verdicts that flatter their side — *even when wrong*) raises their trust: shortcuts open, their witnesses speak freely, their vault discounts. But the kingdom only fully restores on *truth*, not loyalty — flattering verdicts cap at Bronze. Mid-game pivot: the player realizes trust is a tool and truth is the win condition; max ending requires completing the **Reconciliation Stone** case (if the feud's pattern actually ended — computable) or the **Eternal Stalemate** plaque (if not). Playing both sides is allowed, noticed, and commented on by the Archivist.

## 11. Discovery Systems

- **Evidence inventory:** every found item is a card (fragment, sundial reading, tally-stone, conceded testimony) usable across cases — old evidence sometimes cracks a *later* case ("wait, that fragment mentioned the Night District…"), rewarding hoarders and re-readers.
- **Excavation:** 3-swipe brush minigame, 5 seconds, particle payoff.
- **Interrogation:** press with evidence to force concessions; catching a lie rings a bell kingdom-wide (public, satisfying).
- **Reconstruction:** the author-assignment puzzle (the crown jewel — real excerpts as puzzle pieces).
- **The Codex:** silhouetted until earned; entries store the case's full true record (the analytics data, fully earned, never dumped).

## 12. Quest Systems

- **Meta-case — "The Great Silence" (the spine):** the clocktower's five great slots (*What was the last thing said before the Silence? Who said it? How long did the dark last? Who broke it? With what?*) can only be filled with verdict-seals earned from district cases. Every ordinary case visibly feeds the big one — a progress constellation on the clock face.
- **District cases:** 8–12 generated monument cases (the core loop).
- **Faction cases:** contested-march cases with double testimony.
- **Figure cases (side):** one per echo (e.g., The Vanished: deduce when they left and when they returned from absence data + peaks).
- **Micro-cases:** Joke Shrine decodes, knowledge-gate vaults — 30–60s snacks between mains.

## 13. Achievement Systems

- **Player (effort/skill):** *First Stamp*, *Gold Rush* (3 Gold seals in a row), *Perjury!* (catch 5 lies), *Profiler* (perfect Reconstruct with zero misplacements), *Cartographer* (100% ink), *Double Agent* (max both factions' trust), *Heartkeeper* (finale), *Fact-Checker* (find the deliberately mislabeled stele).
- **Kingdom (the group's records as world wonders, on plaques):** *The Thousand-Voice Riot*, *The 47-Day Silence*, *The Age of Gold* — each doubles as a share card.

## 14. Secret Content Design

- **The Moon Door:** opened only via The Sentinel's questline → the Night District Under the District, built from the group's 0–5 AM messages; its case has the strangest reconstruction in the game.
- **The Vanished's trail:** unmarked case; the only clue is an empty plinth.
- **The mislabeled stele:** one monument's inscription is deterministically "wrong"; correcting it via verdict earns *Fact-Checker* + a codex rare. Teaches distrust of plaques — i.e., teaches detective mindset.
- **The Undercroft:** 100% codex unlocks the room beneath the Heart holding the chat's **first message ever**, enshrined in gold.
- **Edge shrine:** walk the map border 30s → "Here ends what was written."

## 15. Nostalgia & Emotional Payoff Design

- **Iron rule:** fiction outside, real messages inside — but in v5 the real messages are *puzzle pieces first, payoff second*: the player has studied, placed, and bet on every line before it's confirmed real. Earned nostalgia hits harder than displayed nostalgia.
- **Escalation:** comedy cases first (falafel wars, emoji carnivals) → mid-game weight (the Silence, The Vanished, conversation graveyards) → warmth (the Revival case crowns the member who actually restarted the chat) → the Heart relights.
- **The Procession finale (kept):** every restored monument and figure parades to the Heart in era order through streets the player rebuilt — verdict seals carried like banners. Ends on the Kingdom Card.

## 16. Shareability Design

All client-rendered, no network (reuses the share-card pipeline; note the open export bug in SHARE_CARD_EXPORT_BUG_HANDOFF.md — fixing it benefits both features):
- **Kingdom Card** (finale): map silhouette, era banners, restoration %, Detective Rank, gold-seal count.
- **Case files** (per solved case): monument art + the real excerpt + the player's seal — the viral unit ("we built a monument to THIS").
- **Figure cards** (per member): statue, archetype, evidence line — taggable; the strongest pull for other members to install and play.
- **Verdict challenges:** a case's empty slots as a shareable card ("Can you solve The Shattering? I got Silver.") — score-comparison without any backend.

## 17. Long-Term Retention Design

- **Persistence:** localStorage keyed by chat hash + season (pattern of `cw_duoquest_v1`): seals, codex, trust, map ink, evidence inventory.
- **Completionism:** Gold-seal hunting (re-attempt stamped cases for grade, once per real day — gentle return ritual, deterministic content), secrets, full codex — 2–3× main-arc playtime.
- **New export = the kingdom grows:** re-export months later → a new district rises at the edge with fresh cases; the Heart dims slightly until restored. Structural retention a timeline can never offer.
- **Social loop:** every member who receives a Figure card or Verdict challenge is a new player with the same deterministic kingdom — and veterans compete on seals.

## 18. Complete Player Journey

1. **0:00** — Menu tile: *The Ruins of [Group]*, dark beacon. Cold open: the Archivist slides over an empty case book. "The records are sealed. The factions lie. You're new — nobody will suspect you of having a side."
2. **0:30** — Gates district. A scorched plaza with three empty verdict slots glowing. "…what happened here?" — inside the first minute.
3. **1:30** — Tutorial case solved (one excavation, one bubble placed, one stamp): first ✓ flip, first restoration, lanterns relight, **three leads fan out across the fogged map**. First win < 90s, and the case book is already fuller than when it started.
4. **3–10 min** — Two district cases; first interrogation; first caught lie (bell rings); first faction overture ("stamp it our way, friend"). The Heart visible, dark, far.
5. **10–18 min** — The Ashen Reach blocks the way; the clocktower's five great slots revealed — every solved case now visibly feeds the constellation. Trust games in the Marches.
6. **18–26 min** — Crossing the Reach; the Silence and Revival cases; the crowning of the reviver; the choice at the Reconciliation Stone.
7. **26–30 min** — The Heart relights → Procession → Kingdom Card → share sheet → codex at 58%… "what's behind the Moon Door?"
8. **Returns** — Gold-seal reruns, secrets, Figure cards into the group chat, and months later: "your kingdom has grown."

## Out of scope (per the brief)

No implementation tasks, architecture, or roadmap. Feasibility anchors only: deterministic signal table (§9), existing canvas/i18n/persistence precedents, 4 new detectors. Graceful degradation: small/quiet chats → fewer peaks → smaller kingdom with fewer cases (floor: one district + tutorial case + the Heart) — never an empty state. Open product decisions for later: whether the old quiz survives anywhere (recommendation: no — interrogations/verdicts absorb it), and ad-gate placement for this mode.
